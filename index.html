Powered by research

Built on how people actually learn.

Klaro isn't built on hunches. Each part of the study view follows findings that learning research has tested again and again.

Research · Multimedia learning

Keep the source next to the explanation.

People learn better when related words and pictures sit together, instead of apart on different pages or screens.

So the book page sits beside the lecture, at the moment it's explained.

Ginns (2006). Integrating information: a meta-analysis of the spatial contiguity and temporal contiguity effects. Learning and Instruction.

Research · Retrieval practice

Testing yourself beats reading it again.

Students who practised recalling what they'd read remembered more a week later than students who studied it again.

So your course comes with real past-paper questions to practise on.

Roediger & Karpicke (2006). Test-enhanced learning: taking memory tests improves long-term retention. Psychological Science.

Research · Feedback

Know what you got wrong, and where to look.

Feedback is one of the strongest influences on learning, when it tells learners how they did and what to work on next.

So each answer shows the right one, with the book page and lecture clip that explain it.

Hattie & Timperley (2007). The power of feedback. Review of Educational Research.
